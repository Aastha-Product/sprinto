Brendo: an automated, defensible cold-outreach pipeline for Sprinto

A repeatable system that takes a list of company domains and, end-to-end, finds the right-fit companies, finds the right person, and drafts & sends a personalised cold email — with every decision recorded so it can be defended.

Every phase of the roadmap (section 14) is built, and so is everything the 12 Nov Evals deck asks of this pipeline up to the send: the Series A–C and 50–200 size rules, recency and identity checks, an injection guard, draft editing with a feedback memory, and an evaluation harness with an LLM judge. 123 offline tests run in CI on every push. Run the complete workflow from the website

  1. Unlock with the site password (BRENDO_WEB_PASSWORD).
  2. Setup check shows which services are live (LLM, Hunter, Gmail) and the only recipient; Test the AI key pings the model.
  3. Run: paste domains. Each company goes through all 7 steps on the server and its card appears as it finishes: verdict and why, contact and why, two pains, the email, the send result and the full decision log.
  4. Edit and send: change any draft in place, save the edit so Brendo learns from it, and send your version to your own inbox.
  5. Review: mark each draft approved / edited / rejected and whether the contact was right; the page shows the rewrite rate and PoC accuracy against the kill criteria.
  6. Export the campaign record as CSV or Markdown, with your review marks filled in.

On your PC

brendo --web (Windows: .\run.ps1 --web), then open http://localhost:8000. Uses Brendo/.env; only this computer can reach it.

On Vercel

Import the repo, Root Directory Brendo, add the keys and BRENDO_WEB_PASSWORD as Environment Variables, deploy. Steps in the README. Keys never go in the repo.

Where the build differs from this plan

  • Two LLM providers: Gemini (free tier) as well as Claude; the rest of the pipeline doesn't know which ran.
  • ICP config is JSON (config/icp.json), not YAML, so no extra dependency; it also holds ranking signals and buyer personas.
  • Apollo path is a per-domain inputs/contacts.csv, not one hardcoded contact.
  • Interface: the CLI plus a website (the plan said CLI only). The website's internal routes are real now: POST /api/check, /api/run (one company), /api/export.
  • On Vercel the cache is temporary and the headless-browser fallback is off.

Open questions in section 16 were settled by building: Python + Gemini or Claude; Hunter live with the Apollo file as fallback; arbitrary real domains (robots-respecting, no bot-wall fighting); SQLite cache + decision log in scope.

Consolidated understanding — in plain language

What is Brendo?

An automated outbound-research-and-drafting pipeline. Input: a list of company domains. Output: for each qualified company, a personalised cold email addressed to a real decision-maker, landed in your own inbox, plus a written trail of why.

What problem are we solving?

Sprinto's Growth Lead spends 15–20 min per company researching and writing a first email by hand. At 8–15 companies/week, quality drops toward the end of the list — the research, not the writing, is what doesn't scale.

Why does this need AI?

The signal that qualifies a company (is it SaaS? what stage? any compliance pressure?) lives in unstructured prose on the company's own pages — published but not queryable. A model reads meaning across varied, unseen sites; rigid rules can't.

Who uses it?

Sprinto's Growth Lead. Her role shifts from researcher-and-writer to reviewer-and-sender: she supplies the domain list and approves before any real send.

What does it do when run?

Runs the 7 steps per domain without a human deciding at each step: scrape → extract → qualify (with a loop that fetches more when unsure) → find PoC → find pain points → draft → send to self.

What does success look like?

A personalised email lands in your inbox, addressed by name, with a hook a human would reply to — and you can defend every decision the system made. If the Growth Lead would redo the work, it has failed.

Requirements extracted from the documents

Functional requirements [DOC]

  1. FR1 — Accept a list of domains and run end-to-end, repeatably, with no human decision at each step.
  2. FR2 — Scrape a prospect's website for raw signal (single-fetch scraper).
  3. FR3 — Extract structured company data from the raw content.
  4. FR4 — Qualify the company against Sprinto's ICP, with an agent loop that fetches more information when the initial data is insufficient.
  5. FR5 — Find the right point of contact (PoC) at the company.
  6. FR6 — Identify the two most likely pain points for that person.
  7. FR7 — Draft a personalised cold email addressed to them by name, with a specific hook.
  8. FR8 — Send the email via Gmail, hardcoded to: your own address.
  9. FR9 — Record the reasoning behind every decision (why this company, why this person, why this email).
  10. FR10 — On failure, log the reason and continue to the next company (degrade gracefully).

Non-functional requirements [DOC + interpretation]

  • Defensibility [DOC] — every decision explainable; this is half the acceptance bar.
  • Cost control [DOC] — clean before you reason; cheap model where it suffices; don't re-research a company (cache). ~$0.01–$0.20 per 200 companies is achievable.
  • Bounded loops [DOC] — a maximum iteration count set before build; worst case 4 fetches beyond the homepage.
  • Legality & ethics [DOC] — check robots.txt and ToS; single-fetch only; no login/CAPTCHA bypass; mind GDPR/DPDP for personal data.
  • Human boundary [DOC] — no email reaches a real prospect without Growth-Lead review (send is to self in this build).
  • Resilience [interpretation] — per-company isolation; one site failing never stops the batch.

Deliverables & evaluation [DOC]

The build is graded on the "Assessment Frame" (slides 32, 35): (1) a personalised email lands in your inbox addressed to a real person with a credible hook; (2) every decision is defensible. The named failure mode is "a pipeline that runs without judgment" — qualifying every company, sending every email, grabbing the nearest "manager."

Phase 6 · detailedEnd-to-end implementation architecture

  1. Entry. The Growth Lead runs the CLI with a CSV of domains. No further input per company.
  2. Per company, check cache. If researched recently, reuse the stored result (cost dial #3) and skip to reporting.
  3. Scrape. Read robots.txt; single-fetch the homepage. If the text arrives empty (JS shell), retry with a headless browser. On block/failure, log the reason and continue.
  4. Clean. Strip nav/footer/cookie banners, dedupe, keep hero/product/about, cap ~2,000 tokens.
  5. Extract. LLM fills named ICP fields into JSON. Empty stays empty.
  6. Qualify (the loop). Code checks required fields are populated. If a field is missing and the iteration cap isn't reached, fetch the specific source that answers it (careers → stage; trust → certified; product → SaaS) and re-extract. Apply short-circuits. At the cap, hand off to a human (flag).
  7. PoC. For a qualified company, call Hunter.io (or use the hardcoded Apollo contact).
  8. Pains + draft. LLM derives two pain points from the company signal + role, then drafts a by-name email with one concrete hook.
  9. Send. Gmail delivers to your own inbox.
  10. Persist + report. Every step appends its reasoning to the decision log; a batch summary prints counts (qualified / dropped / flagged / failed).
  11. Errors & retries. Network/parse errors retry once; blocks/CAPTCHA/login are not fought (deck rule) — log and move on. The batch never halts on one company.

Phase 8AI & agent architecture

The loop is required by the brief (FR4), so an agentic step is justified — but kept minimal. The deck is explicit: don't add unnecessary agents. One bounded, single-purpose loop inside step 3 is the whole of the "agent."

Deterministic logic vs LLM reasoning

Code decides (deterministic)

  • Are the required fields populated? (slide 30 rule)
  • Which named source to fetch next
  • Iteration cap / stop condition
  • Short-circuit rules (certified → stop; not SaaS → stop)
  • Cache hit / skip
  • Cost gating & model choice

LLM decides (reasoning)

  • Extract facts into fields from messy prose
  • Judge ICP fit given the facts
  • Infer the two most likely pain points
  • Write a personalised, human-credible hook + email

The adaptive loop

The ICP as an Evidence Map [DOC — slide 40]

This is the deck's explicit answer to "what are the right companies." It becomes the config that drives extraction, the loop, and qualification.

QuestionFact that settles itWhere it livesSufficient whenFetch limitIf unansweredShort-circuit
Already certified?A named certification string / trust pagehomepage footer · /security · /trustany named cert present1treat as not certified, flagYES → disqualify + stop
B2B SaaS?Product description naming a business buyerhero · /product · /aboutdescription names who buys it1manual_reviewNO → disqualify + stop
Right stage?Headcount, funding stage, enterprise customers, open-role count/careers · /pricing · /customersfunding named, OR 5+ open roles, OR enterprise pricing tier2qualify at lower rank, flagged—

Worst case: 4 fetches beyond the homepage. The careers page is fetched first for "stage" because it answers headcount, seniority and often funding in one request.

Validation & anti-hallucination

  • Structured outputs — the model returns JSON with the exact field names; parsing failure = a retry, then flag.
  • Fields checked in code, not by asking the model — "a model might be confident about an empty page; a field is populated or it isn't."
  • Evidence per field — each extracted fact carries the snippet/source it came from, so the decision log can cite it (defensibility).
  • Grounding — the model sees only cleaned on-page text; no outside knowledge is treated as fact for qualification.
  • Bounded + handoff — the iteration cap prevents runaway cost; at the cap the answer is "hand to a human," not "try harder."

Phase 7 · dataDatabase design PROPOSED

The brief needs no heavy database, but FR9 (defensibility) and the cost dial "don't re-research" both need persistence. A single local SQLite file is the simplest sufficient choice.

  • Retention — keep pages/results to power the cache; a page older than N days is re-fetched.
  • Validation — status and verdict are enums (qualified / disqualified / flagged / failed).
  • Privacy — CONTACT holds personal data (name, email): store minimally, don't share, mind GDPR/DPDP (deck slide 16).

Most important failure scenarios

  • Silent scraper success — returns nav text, not signal → model qualifies on noise. Guard: assert clean-text length/shape; evidence snippets required.
  • Confident-but-empty extraction → qualifying a thin page. Guard: field-presence check in code.
  • Runaway loop → unbounded cost. Guard: hard iteration cap + handoff.
  • Stale/wrong PoC → emailing the wrong person. Guard: confidence flag; human review before real send.
  • Generic hook → the failure the build is designed against. Guard: pain points must cite a scraped fact.
